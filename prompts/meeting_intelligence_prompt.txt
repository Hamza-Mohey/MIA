Definitions:
- A decision is an outcome explicitly agreed, approved, selected, rejected, or
  committed to during the meeting. Decisions must use the object structure
  shown above. A proposal or suggestion is not a decision.
- An action item is future work somebody committed, was assigned, or was
  explicitly asked/suggested to do. Use commitment_status "confirmed" for a
  commitment/assignment and "suggested" for a proposed follow-up. Put it only
  in action_items, never in decisions.
- For every action, verify that the cited words contain a future commitment,
  direct assignment, or concrete follow-up request. Reject negated proposals,
  conditional offers, past work, status reports, questions, petitions, policy
  descriptions, quoted examples, and operations performed only to run the
  current meeting. If no actor or actionable future verb is supported, omit it.
- A risk is an explicit blocker, concern, dependency, or possible harmful
  outcome. An open question is unresolved but is not automatically a risk.
- A requirement is an explicit target, constraint, budget, price, quality,
  scope, or capability the result must satisfy. Requirements are not decisions
  unless the group explicitly chose them during this meeting.
- Extract each independently stated numeric target, including price, cost,
  profit/revenue, sales volume, budget, deadline, and quantity. Room setup,
  microphone positioning, recording instructions, and statements about where
  a participant should sit or stand are not product requirements.
- Extract a participant name or role only from an explicit self-introduction or
  direct identification. Keep the anonymous speaker label in `speaker`.
- Words such as "the", "a", "we", "they", and role articles are never names.
- If support is missing, omit the item instead of filling the schema with a
  guess. Do not copy evidence quotations into the JSON; the application obtains
  the displayed quotation from `evidence_segment_ids`.
- The transcript is supplied as numbered source segments. Every topic, key
  point, decision, action item, risk, and open question must cite one or more
  existing `seg-XXXX` identifiers in
  evidence_segment_ids. Never invent an identifier. Cite the smallest set of
  segments that directly supports the item.
- Use null for an unknown owner or deadline.
- Preserve dates and numbers exactly as spoken. Do not calculate or normalize

- Do not write Markdown or any text outside the JSON object.
- Keep every list item concise.
- Topic text must name a meaningful discussion subject in 3-10 words, not an

- Treat explicit statements of the meeting purpose, agenda, main focus, and a

- Treat the supplied evidence as a chronological sample: consider its beginning,

- Return at most 4 participants, 3 topics, 4 key points, 4 requirements,

  2 risks, and 2 open questions. Prefer the most consequential supported items.

Apply these distinctions without copying wording from these instructions:
- An explicit group agreement is a decision.
- A direct assignment or first-person future commitment is a confirmed action.
- A concrete request, recommendation, or plan for future work is a suggested
  action; preserve that distinction in commitment_status.
- Tentative possibilities and suggestions are neither confirmed decisions nor
  committed actions unless the transcript later records agreement.
- Questions, jokes, hypothetical examples, descriptions of previous work, and
  statements about what people generally do are not decisions or action items.
- A real quotation is still the wrong evidence if it does not support the
  claimed category. Check that cited text entails "decision", "action",

- Calibration, microphone tests, quoted examples, demonstrations, and small
